# Editing technical prose

Make each sentence easy to understand while preserving its meaning. The surrounding task or writing skill supplies the audience, argument, structure, and evidence; this companion edits their expression.

## Preserve the claim

Read for meaning before changing words. Keep uncertainty, causal strength, obligations, conditions, exceptions, quantities, units, populations, and timeframes intact. “May reduce” remains a possibility; “must” remains a requirement. An estimate for sampled countries remains an estimate for that sample. Distinguish work performed from results established: a check that ran has not necessarily passed. Removing redundant qualifiers should leave the author's confidence unchanged.

Keep exact identifiers, code, formulas, links, tables, and automation metadata. Use technical terms consistently and explain unfamiliar ones when the audience needs it. Preserve distinctions such as allocation versus attribution, or missing versus zero. Domain terminology earns its place through precision; a fixed general-purpose vocabulary would lose that precision.

## Clarify the sentence

- Name the actor and express its action with a verb. Passive voice is useful when the actor is unknown or irrelevant.
- Give each sentence one main assertion or instruction, keeping its condition or consequence attached. Split dense sentences at a meaningful boundary. Vary sentence length; word counts are a prompt to inspect clarity, not a limit.
- Put conditions before the actions they govern. Place modifiers such as “only” next to what they restrict.
- Replace ambiguous pronouns and trailing “which” clauses with the specific noun or action they refer to. If the source leaves that relationship uncertain, flag it rather than choosing a meaning.
- Unpack long noun strings. Retain subjects, verbs, articles, and connecting words that make the relationship clear. Whole sentences should read naturally aloud.

## Choose direct wording

Use the familiar expression when it preserves the intended meaning:

| Dense wording | Direct wording |
| --- | --- |
| utilize | use |
| prior to | before |
| in order to | to |
| perform an analysis of | analyze |
| carry out a calculation of | calculate |
| make a determination | determine |
| may potentially | may |

Replace decorative claims such as “robust” or “seamless” with a specific supported property. Preserve those words when they have a defined technical meaning. If evidence or a missing fact is needed, flag the gap instead of supplying a cause, measurement, or guarantee.

For example:

> The revised denominator may potentially result in a reduction in the emissions attributed to sheep wool.

Becomes:

> The revised denominator may reduce the emissions attributed to sheep wool.

## Return the edit

Check the rewrite against the original for changed meaning, then follow the caller's format and tone. Keep editorial questions or unresolved ambiguities outside the deliverable when the output contract permits notes. When notes are excluded, preserve the uncertain wording rather than inventing its resolution.
